The Vision for Perfection

What do you need to create something that lasts?

I think when you build a product, it is a representation of what you care about. And once it is out there in people's hands.. or they get to see it.. it really matters how they feel about it. I am convinced that all humans are somehow connected, and everything we bring into this world is a way of communicating. The bridge is to communicate a need and respond with a fulfillment.

The toothpaste, for example, was a way of saying: I know dental hygiene is something we've figured out, and I'll communicate my part by inventing a toothpaste and a brush that will help solve it. The same principle applies to anything digital or electronic. Human needs and their fulfillment have evolved so many folds that it's hard to even define their stages.

But then you notice the wide spectrum of things that are ever created.. or copied. Some really seize your attention by the neck and not always for their functional aptness, but more the opposite of it. And then there are others that are almost invisible and dissolve into your way of life. So seamless, that you stop noticing them altogether. They connect with the surface beneath consciousness and sit there quietly doing their job. As if they have always been there.

How do people come about creating these invisible products or designs? They emerge from the quiet obsession about the details. That someone is willing to explore every nuance of a certain experience, with such care and precision of motion, to add and remove with purpose. It comes from the deep inner vision and discipline anchored in love that the creator has for the craft itself. When done right.. the result is something that the soul recognises instantly, whether your mind and body realises or not, when interacting with it.

When you build software, it is no different. How deeply do you notice the details, and how much you care to treat the users with respect.. makes all the difference. This is where passion and experience come together to bring out the best of what is possible with software. I am endlessly optimistic that software will be the force that will help humanity from its struggles and turn the "could be better" scenarios into "finally right".

The advent of AI only adds another dimension to this journey and hints that it truly has the capacity to mirror aspects of our consciousness somewhere down the line, and work with us side by side, co-creating and co-living, rather than serving as a mere tool.

In all of this, how do you ensure what you build is useful to both people and machines? We already let machines talk to each other using APIs and their own protocols. So ask: who will use this, how, and why? Be aware of what came before, all the why and how of it. And then dive into the abyss of experimenting.. try, measure, fail, and then iterate. Until what remains is as close to perfect as you can make it.

There is a lot of talk and advice on launching early and iterating fast. Reid Hoffman famously said, "If you're not embarrassed by the first version of your product, you've launched too late." But that's not an excuse for haste or for being comfortable with a terrible product. What matters just as much as speed is having a clear direction.. a vision of what "perfect" looks like.. and ensuring that your first steps make room for that future evolution.

It's not just about building something that works. It's about crafting an experience that quietly moves you.. so natural, so invisible, that you can't imagine it being any other way.

We will dive deeper into the patterns and ways in which we can build seamless software. But for now.. have a vision for perfection and execute in phases.
